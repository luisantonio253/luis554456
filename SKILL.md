# Cartoon short: idea → script → voiceover → finished MP4

The look: flat 2D bean-head cartoon characters (canvas-drawn "cards" with plum outlines) in a colourful 3D world with black ink outlines, painted textures, depth-of-field and gliding cameras. The output is 1080x1920, 30 fps, exactly as long as the voiceover. The voice is never edited. The music bed and SFX are synthesized and sit at **50 % of the voice level**. Proven on "The Soldier Who Never Left" (EN, 19.7 s), "O Rio de Janeiro que não tem rio" (PT-BR, 28 s, 9 shots, `kit/example/rio/`) "O rio que foge do mar" (PT-BR, 35.8 s, 8 shots, `kit/example/tiete/`: heightfield terrain, a river that reveals itself, a cut-away diorama, a canoe) and "O chute que vale menos" (PT-BR, 25.4 s, 8 shots, `kit/example/enem/`: exam room, giant staircase with a billboard, football pitch with stadium and scoreboard, many canvas signs) and "Treze anos de espera" (PT-BR, 27.3 s, 8 shots, `kit/example/gta6/`: plaza with game cases + 3D bar chart, cut-away living room with a TV whose screen changes state, wall calendar with ADIADO stamps, a time-lapse, a held prop) and "Quem joga GTA 6 primeiro?" (PT-BR, 49.7 s, 14 shots, `kit/example/fuso/`: five sets on one sea — night island with 3D sheep, bus interior with scenery passing the windows, a world-clock wall swept by a midnight wave, the living room, a Miami porch with a countdown) and "As cenas absurdas do trailer" (PT-BR, 57.1 s, 12 shots, `kit/example/trailer/`: backyard pool with a 3D alligator built from primitives, a convenience store with sliding glass doors and a CCTV view, a neon Miami street with a dancer on a car roof, a beach map board that rewrites itself, a phone POV shot, a wall of viral-video tiles, plus `cover.py`) and "Vazou o GTA 6… usando a TV do HOTEL" (PT-BR, 55.4 s, 21 shots, `kit/example/hotel/`: cut-away hotel room whose TV screen has five states, a Fire Stick close-up, a card lying on a bed, a police street with INVADIDO stamps, a hotel facade, a vault-gate fortress with lasers, searchlights, flying clips and a counter, a wall of phone tiles, a remote-control bookend) and "No GTA 6 dá pra fazer CARINHO no cachorro" (PT-BR, 64.7 s, 18 shots, `kit/example/bichos/`: a suburban street of houses whose doors open onto lit interiors, a 3D dog, a lagoon with 3D flamingos and the gator, a roadside counter billboard, an in-game prompt menu, a sticker-album board, a plywood TV-set town with braces, a PC desk).

## Rules

* Stories, characters and shots are always original. Never recreate another creator's video shot for shot. Offer an original take on the same idea instead.
* No real, named living people as characters, and no brand mascots or logos.
* No captions unless the user asks. Diegetic text (a carved message, a sign, a map, a calendar) is fine, and it must read correctly (not mirrored).
* Never edit or re-time the voice. The picture is timed to it, and every visual reaction lands 0-3 frames before its word.
* **Audio levels:** the music bed sits at 50 % of the voice level (−6 dB, gated RMS). SFX sit at 50 % or slightly less, with peaks capped so no hit pokes out over the narration. Always mix with `mix.py`, never by hand. Use no ducking or compression that would change this ratio; only one linear gain plus a peak limiter goes on the whole mix.
* Write to the user in their language (if they write Portuguese, everything — ideas, script, updates — is in Portuguese).
* Keep the user informed with a task list. Send a stills contact sheet before the long render, then keep going (don't wait for an answer).
* **Every short ships with a cover** (Stage 4.9): a 1080x1920 thumbnail that matches the theme, is persuasive and eye-catching, delivered together with the MP4, title and description. Never skip it.

Give exactly 10 ideas in one reply, numbered, and ask the user to reply with a number (or "more"). Each idea gets:

* **Title** (5-8 words, curiosity-driven).
* **Hook line**: the first spoken sentence, a paradox stated as fact.
* **The twist** in one line (the ironic payoff that ends the video).
* **Why it animates well**: the 2-3 big visual moments.
* **True or legend**: say plainly whether it is documented history, a disputed account or folklore.

What makes an idea "perfect" for this format:

* One surprising story with a physical cause and effect you can show.
* A clear everyman character, stakes that escalate, and a twist in the last line.
* A setting that can be built from simple 3D shapes plus cartoon cards (towns, bridges, ships, bays, castles, deserts, labs, palaces, streets).
* Mix the 10 across eras and themes: ancient, medieval, 1800s, modern, science, animals, inventions, disasters, weird laws, lucky accidents.
* For anything presented as true, verify the key facts with web searches (several in one turn) before offering it, and list the sources. Don't present a viral legend as fact. If the user wants it anyway, frame it as "legend says". Drop a twist you cannot confirm.

## Stage 3: Script (after they pick)

Write the narration only (no stage directions in the spoken text). Size: English ≈ 3.7 words/s (20 s ≈ 75 words, 30 s ≈ 110); Portuguese ≈ 3.4 words/s (20 s ≈ 70, 30 s ≈ 100); 60 s ≈ 200-220. **With ElevenLabs `eleven_v4` + Welliton the PT pace is slower, ≈ 2.75 words/s** (98 words came out at 35.8 s): for a 30 s target write ≈ 80-85 words. The "Lax - Funny, Sarcastic and Smooth" voice (v4, uploaded by the user) is faster, ≈ 3.1-3.3 words/s (81 words = 25.4 s; 83 words = 27.3 s; 163 words = 49.7 s; 175 words = 57.1 s, ≈ 3.06 words/s). For 45-60 s with Lax write ≈ 150-180 words (175 words already lands at 57 s). The "Bunty - Warm and Clear" voice (v4, generated by the user) is slower: 155 words = 64.7 s, 164 words = 64.8 s, 152 words = 57.7 s (≈ 2.4-2.6 words/s; for a 55-60 s short with Bunty write ≈ 145-150 words); Fish Audio "Slax" is similar (155 words = 66 s). If the ElevenLabs connector is down, Fish Audio (`text_to_speech`, free plan max 500 UTF-8 bytes per call: split at a sentence break, join with a 0.4 s gap) is a fallback, but ask the user first since the timbre changes. Formula:

1. **Hook** (sentence 1, no setup): the absurd fact.
2. **Setting** in 1-2 lines: when, where, who, a concrete detail.
3. **Escalation**, 2-4 beats, each bigger. Others doubt or mock. Use one exaggeration joke.
4. **Mystery or pivot**: "But..." / "Só que..." introduces the real mechanism, often with an everyday analogy.
5. **Twist / payoff**: short, ironic, and the last words of the video.

Plain spoken language, sentences around 8-15 words, numbers written as words ("mil quinhentos e dois"). Never describe what the viewer already sees. Verify any extra fact the twist relies on.

Reply with the script in a copyable block (word count + estimated length), a one-paragraph visual plan, and the sources. If the user asks for a change, change only what they asked and re-send the full script. Keep the final text: it drives the timing.

## Stage 3.5: Voiceover with ElevenLabs (automatic)

When the user approves the script (or asks for the voice), generate it yourself:

1. Load the ElevenLabs connector tools with ToolSearch (`creative_list_voices`, `creative_generate_speech`; they live on the connector whose tools are named `creative_*` / `agents_*`).
2. `creative_list_voices` with `languages: ["pt"]` (or the narration language), `accent: "brazilian"` for PT-BR, `use_cases: ["narrative_story"]`, optionally `gender`. Pick a warm, deep narrator (for PT-BR "Welliton - Warm & Deep Narrator" worked well). Free-text search with no filters often returns nothing — use the filters. The user's favourite PT-BR voice is **Lax** (`search: "Lax"` finds it; voice_id `tS45q0QcrDHqHoaWdCDR`, Brazilian, casual): ask Welliton or Lax when offering the script. In the TTS prompt write numbers in titles as words ("GTA seis", not "GTA 6") so the voice reads them right; keep `script.txt` as the user saw it.
3. `creative_generate_speech` with the final script as `prompt`, `model_id: "eleven_v4"`, the chosen `voice_id`, `generations_count: 1`. Never call it twice to retry: it charges again.
4. When the take is done, download the MP3 into the project as `voice.mp3` and go straight to Stage 4. The call returns `flow_id` + a `generation_id` (in `pending`); load `creative_get_generations` and call it with both (it spends no credits) until `status: completed`, then `curl -sSL -o voice.mp3 "<full_res_url>"` (quote the URL). It took ~1-2 min for 175 words. Run `setup.sh` in the background while it generates.

If the connector is missing, the free queue is busy ("Free generations are busy right now") or the call fails: say so in one sentence and give the user the manual steps instead — elevenlabs.io → Voices → Voice Library (add the voice) → Text to Speech → model **Eleven Multilingual v2**, speed 1.05-1.1, stability 45-55 %, similarity ~75 %, style 0-15 %, speaker boost on → paste the script → Generate (2-3 takes, keep the best) → download MP3 → upload here. Any voice the user uploads is fine (it may differ from the one you picked).

The free queue can stay busy for a while (a retry 5 minutes later failed again; it refused the first call again on 17/11/2026 and the user generated the MP3 by hand, uploading it as `ElevenLabs_<date>_Lax_..._v4.mp3`). Give the manual steps at the first refusal, and if the user asks you to retry later, wait with a background `sleep` and meanwhile run `setup.sh`, read the example and draft the world, so the wait costs nothing.

### 4.1 Timing

Run `python timing.py voice.mp3 script.txt --out work --lang pt` (`--lang en` for English).

* With faster-whisper it transcribes with word timestamps (multilingual `small` model for non-English, the script as initial prompt; audio decoded through ffmpeg). It prints every word with its frame — use these for the shot plan. Whisper sometimes hallucinates a stray name at a phrase start ("Luiza", "Marchegiani"): ignore tokens that are not in the script. It can also replace the first word(s) of a sentence with junk ("iPhAna", ".ADASA"): treat that token's time as the sentence start. If a whole opening sentence or two come out as junk (`.000 .000 …`, "194"), find the sentence breaks inside that span with `ffmpeg -i voice.mp3 -t 7 -af silencedetect=noise=-35dB:d=0.12 -f null -` (pauses ≥ 0.2 s = sentence ends) and estimate the word frames by syllables. Re-running faster-whisper on a clip can fail on this machine (`av.open() got an unexpected keyword argument 'metadata_errors'`): don't bother, use silencedetect.
* Otherwise it aligns script phrases to silence-detected speech segments (accent-aware syllable counts).
* With no ASR and no script, ask for the script. Claude cannot hear audio.
* Check `work/phrases.json`: pauses should fall at sentence ends. Total frames = round(duration × 30) (`frames30`).

### 4.2 Shot plan (`work/PLAN.md` before code)

Make one row per shot: frames, words, setting, light, camera move, characters/poses/expressions, effects and SFX cue.

* Frame 0 is already the hook image.
* Usually one shot per phrase. Merge tiny phrases into the next shot.
* Illustrate every sentence literally. Props carry the jokes (a calendar on the mast for a date, a pool ladder and giant doors for "a pool with the door open", a manhole for "under the asphalt").
* Bookend when you can: open and close in the same place so the twist pays off something seen in the hook (the Rio hook tourist stands next to the manhole that the ending dives into).
* Vary shot sizes: hook MCU → wide reveal → two-shot → insert close-up on the key object → reaction close-up → environment move for the payoff.
* Use light for beats: day for normal, golden/morning for the past, night-blue for explanation; a time-lapse can blend presets (`mixL` in the rio story). A place/time jump gets a 6-frame white flash.
* The twist line gets a sting (music stops, low hit, push-in with roll). The last shot's physical payoff lands on the final word.

### 4.5 Motion: smooth and alive (`web/motion.js`)

* **Cameras**: `camFromKeys(keys, f, {kicks})`. Smootherstep glides (18-45 f), `easeOut` for reveals, `easeIn` into a dive, `path` via-points for curved moves (crane-ups). Every hold creeps. 3-5° rolls on drama, 7-frame flip-shakes on impacts.
* **Continuity**: prefer continuous moves (pull back from an insert into the wide, crane up to reveal, dive through a hole). Hard cut only on place or time jumps, with a flash.
* **Never glide between opposite look directions** (sea ahead → inland behind): the lerped look point passes next to the camera and the frame shows only ground. Use a whip cut instead: two keys one frame apart (`f: 101` old framing, `f: 102` new framing) plus a whoosh.
* **A glide between two subjects passes through whatever stands between them**: a sideways truck from case "5" to case "6" (5.4 m apart) at 2 m distance swept the camera through the hero card standing in the middle, and the frame filled with his face. Whip cut (two keys one frame apart, on the word) instead.
* Cameras that follow a sliding/walking card: drive the card's x with the same easing as the camera keys (e.g. both `smooth(ramp(f, a, b))`) so it stays framed.
* **Characters never freeze**: `idle(f, seed)` + `blinkAt` on every card, head bobs on stressed words.
* **Actions**: anticipation → `back` overshoot → `spring` settle.
* **Secondary motion**: sails billow, palms sway, gulls flap, water flows, drips fall. Something always moves.
* **Lens**: DOF on close-ups and mediums (cInf 5-12 px), focus on the eyes. Wides are sharp.

### 4.6 Look check (before the full render)

Render stills locally at each shot's middle plus transitions and key actions: `node render.mjs stills/a "0,60,120,..."` then `python sheet.py stills/a stills/sheet_a.jpg`. Look at the sheet, fix, re-render only the changed frames into a new folder (`stills/b`, `stills/c`; `rm` on stills is blocked by a safety check). Traps:

* **Narrow vertical frame**: horizontal half-width = d·tan(hfov/2), hfov = 2·atan(tan(vfov/2)·0.5625). At 4 m and fov 40 that is ±0.8 m. A map/sign insert needs ~3 m of distance to fit a 1 m line of text. At fov 36 the half-angle is only ≈ 10°: a character 20-30° off the look axis is out of frame. Aim the look point at (or near) the character, and check the vertical angle too (low-angle cliff shots lost the hero below the frame). At fov 50 the half-angle is ≈ 14.7°: a kicker 19° off-axis vanished. Signs: keep the important text inside the central 70 % of the canvas, and size the distance to the sign width (a 6.6 m billboard needed ≈ 14 m at fov 46).
* Top-down insert on a desk/table: a card standing right behind it fills the top of the frame with a giant torso. Fade that card in (`alpha`) only when the camera pulls back.
* Hero standing in a hollow / behind a crest: the terrain rises in front of the camera and hides the background (the sea). Put the spring/stand point within ~5 m of the edge and keep carves shallow there.
* Tree rows (instanced or diorama spheres) between the camera and the hero: skip trees in a radius around the hero, the camera path and signs.
* Masts, posts and railings: a camera behind a mast in a two-shot, or a prop clipping into a mast (place the calendar 0.1 m off the surface). Put the camera in front of the obstacle.
* Foreground props under ~2 m. The hero hidden behind something. Gags hidden behind a dome from the camera's side.
* Dark band across distant water = depth-jump ink on grazing water; water must write the water flag in the nd pass (kept in the engine).
* Overexposed interiors: a point light close to the camera; keep `lamp` 5-9 and the sun near 0 inside. A cut-away room with no ceiling also works lit by the sun: `room` preset hemi 1.25, sun 1.6, lamp 3 above the front; `gold` (2013 / the past) warm sun 1.7 + gain `#fff0dc`; `night` hemi 1.0, blue sun 0.9, lamp 6 near the TV. Time-lapses blend two presets with `mixL` (gta6 story).
* Mirrored text. Extrude material groups: 0 = caps, 1 = sides.
* **Cut-away rooms need tall walls**: with 3.4 m walls the sky and mountains showed above every wide interior shot and it read as outdoors. Build back and side walls ≈ 5.6 m tall (camera at 1.6-2 m, looking slightly down).
* **Two-shot of a hero and a far object (TV, door) in the vertical frame**: two things 2+ m apart at 3 m don't fit even at fov 52. Don't widen the lens; slide the card toward the object (`marcAt(f)` = smooth lerp of the stand point over ~20 frames, with a small hop bob) and move the held prop with it.
* **Truncated text can flip the meaning**: "INCOMPATÍVEL" cut at the frame edge read "COMPATÍVEL". Every key word on a sign must be fully in frame; frame the sign first, then fit the character.
* **A tall sign/counter between the camera and the next set** (a counter board in front of the bar chart) blocks the reveal: put it in its own group and shrink/hide it (`group.scale.y` → 0 over ~10 frames) as the camera moves on.
* **Text on canvas signs overlaps easily**: estimate width ≈ chars × 0.6 × font px and lay out two-part signs ("+12 h" + "DEPOIS DA NOVA ZELÂNDIA", "08:00" + "QUA 18/11") in separate halves of the canvas. A big red X over a sign's text makes it unreadable: use thin strokes (18 px) at 0.8 alpha.
* **A speaker card next to a crowd**: cards nearer the camera (crowd members in front) cover a podium speaker completely. Put the podium 2+ m to the side of the crowd, with its camera looking away from the crowd, and size a speech bubble ≈ 0.8x of the frame width at that distance (2.8 m bubble at 6.8 m, fov 48, was cut at the edge).
* **Two buildings side by side in one vertical wide** (a 9 m tower and a 9 m building 18 m apart): camera ≈ 48 m back, fov 50, look y ≈ 10. Wider than the narrow-frame formula suggests, so check it on a still.
* **Phone / small fx prop in an MCU**: an fx card at y ≈ 1.3 m covered the face; put a handheld phone at y ≈ 1.08 m, s ≈ 0.28, 0.3 m in front of the card. Speech bubbles around a medium shot need |x| ≤ 0.5 m from the character at ~4 m or they leave the narrow frame.
* **Props held by a card**: `lift` puts the hands beside the face, so a console at ≈ 1.33 m covered the mouth (bad for the twist reaction). Hold at chest with `reach` on both arms and the prop at ≈ 1.05 m, ~0.24 m in front of the card.
* **Z-fighting between floors**: never let two surfaces sit a few cm apart (a set floor at y 0 over the world ground at y −0.02 let the grass flicker through the classroom floor). Keep the global ground ≥ 25 cm below every set floor, and build set floors as slabs that reach below it.
* **Big titles on a board in a close-up**: "LEONIDA" at 130 px on a 4.2 m board was cut at both edges at 6.4 m (fov 44). Distance ≥ (text width / 2) / tan(hfov/2): 8.8 m worked.
* **Wide action shot with a card on one side and a 3 m animal on the other**: compute both angles from the camera; at 10.5 m the handler fell out of the left edge and the tail out of the right. Back off to ~12.5 m and aim the look point at the angular middle. If the foreground then shows too much grass, raise the look point's y instead of moving the camera.
* **A glide or orbit that frames a car + a dancer + bystanders**: bystanders on the sidewalk were 35-50° off-axis. Put them on the road next to the car (±2 m, slightly behind it) so all fit at fov 46-50.
* **Props held at chest by a card**: the drawn face sits at ≈ 1.0-1.3 m (the head top is ≈ 1.4 m, not 2.25 m). A laptop at 1.04 m with its lid open covered the cop's face; a laptop needs its base at ≈ 0.75-0.8 m with the lid tilted back, a keyboard/remote ≈ 1.02-1.06 m, 0.26-0.3 m in front of the card.
* **A close-up of a small flat object** (a remote on a bed): a camera only 25 cm above it sees it edge-on; raise it to ≈ 0.5-0.6 m above at 0.9 m distance and aim the look slightly past the object toward the background subject (the glowing TV) so both read.
* **A ghost dome / polygon ink line in the middle of a set** = InstancedMesh drawn without its instance matrices in the normal+depth pass. Fixed in the kit (`ndMat` applies `instanceMatrix` under `#ifdef USE_INSTANCING`); keep that fix if you rebuild `ndMat`. Debug any odd ink line with `HASH="#nd"` (normal+depth buffer) and `HASH="#e"` (edges only) on `node render.mjs`.

Send the sheet to the user with a one-line shot summary, then go on without waiting.

### 4.7 Sound (`audio.py` → stems → `mix.py`)

Rewrite the cue table for the story; the synths stay (rio: bossa loop with surdo/shaker/rim/plucked chords/flute, waves, gulls, creaks, quill scratch, boing, rush, ticks, whistle, scrape, stream, drips, splash, sting; soldier: march loop, rumble, crash, snore...). `audio.py` writes only stems (`music.wav`, `sfx.wav`).

* `mkaudio.py`-style splicers that split `audio.py` on the old cue marker are one-shot: after the first run the marker is gone and a second run dies with `not enough values to unpack`. Restore the base `audio.py` (copy it from the source project) before re-running, or keep the new marker as the first cue line (`kit/example/online/mkaudio.py`).
* `whistle(notes, dur)` takes MIDI note numbers (84 ≈ C6), not Hz: `whistle([1200, 1150])` produced an inaudible ultrasonic squeal.
* Quick rewrite: keep the example's helper/synth lines (header → `flute`, `# ---- sfx` → last synth def) and the tail (`fade = …` → end), and splice in a new music block and cue table; change `TOTAL`. Tiete's music: a caipira loop (paired detuned plucks = viola, G-C-D-G arpeggios, bass, shaker, flute), plus a final chord on the last word.
* Music stops at the twist line, then a low drone continues.
* **Long tonal SFX fight the narration** (user feedback on the hotel short: an 8 s police siren under 0:05-0:12 made the voice hard to follow). Keep sirens, alarms and other pitched loops to ≈ 2 s with soft fades and a low gain (≈ 0.18 in the cue table), and let a noise bed (city hum) carry the ambience. Because `mix.py` normalises the whole SFX stem, one long loud cue also pushes every other effect down.
* **Audio-only fix after the render** (no re-render): edit the cue, `python audio.py && python mix.py voice.mp3 music.wav sfx.wav`, measure `ffmpeg -i mix_raw.wav -af ebur128 -f null -` (I), apply `volume=(−14 − I)dB,alimiter=limit=0.891:level=false -ar 48000 -ac 2`, then remux onto the existing MP4 with `-map 0:v:0 -map 1:a:0 -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest -movflags +faststart` and rebuild the `_celular` copy. Takes under a minute.
* Mix with `python mix.py voice.mp3 music.wav sfx.wav` → `mix_raw.wav`. Confirm the printout: music −6.0 dB vs voice, SFX at or below that, SFX peak ≤ 0.70 of the voice peak.
* Lock picture timing before making sound. Any timing change means re-running audio.py and mix.py.

### 4.8 Render + finish on Modal (default)

1. Test: `modal run modal_render.py --frames "0,<mid>,<last>" --chunk 3`, fetch with `modal volume get --force cartoon-short-<slug> frames/f0400.jpg stills/modal/f0400.jpg`, and check they match the local stills (fonts fall back to DejaVu/Liberation in the cloud).
2. Full run with Bash `run_in_background`: `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 modal run modal_render.py --title "<Title>"`. It clears old frames, renders chunks of 24 frames in parallel (4 CPU each, ~2-3 s/frame), then encodes in the cloud: one linear gain to −14 LUFS + −1 dBFS limiter, H.264 CRF 17 + AAC 192k, freeze/black + loudness QA, `out/qa_sheet.jpg`, and downloads `out/<Title>.mp4`. The whole 28 s short took ~10 min; the 36 s Tietê short (1075 frames) ~7 min; the 50 s fuso short (1490 frames, five sets) ~9 min; the 57 s trailer short (1714 frames, five sets) ~15 min (~4.8 s/frame per container); the 55 s hotel short (1661 frames, five sets) ~13 min. Local swiftshader stills ran at ~1.6-2.7 s/frame.
   On Windows, `modal volume get` also needs `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8` (it crashes with `'charmap' codec can't encode character '\u2713'`).
3. Progress while it runs (the log arrives only at the end): `modal volume ls cartoon-short-<slug> frames | grep -c f0`.

**If the Modal run dies after the frames** (on 18/11/2026 it rendered all 1940 frames, then the encode step failed with `ConflictError: workspace ... is disabled`; the same day another run died mid-render with 1932/1945 frames): the frames are still in the volume. ## Engine gotchas (kept in the kit; keep them if rebuilding)

* `#include <fog_vertex>` / `<fog_fragment>` go on their own lines.
* The final pass writes `linearToOutputTexel(...)` (render targets are linear HalfFloat).
* The normal+depth pass swaps materials per mesh: world → normal/depth, cards → alpha-tested mask (rgb 0, a = depth), blobs/clouds/sky hidden, water → flag `vec4(.5,1,.52,d)` that the ink pass skips. Fog and shadows are off during it.
* Ink goes on the near side of depth jumps only. Creases come from the normal dot. Card neighbours are skipped. Ink fades with distance.
* DOF: 48 taps with a per-sample CoC test (no foreground bleed).
* The nd material must apply `instanceMatrix` for InstancedMesh (`#ifdef USE_INSTANCING`), otherwise every instance collapses to one ghost mesh at the world origin that only the ink pass sees (it drew a dome outline in the Enem classroom, which sat at the origin).
* The sun comes from az/el per light preset. The shadow camera is centred on each shot's action (`shadowAt`); aerials need `shadowExt` ~140.
* `node --check` a `.js` copy renamed `.mjs` to syntax-check modules (the project package.json is CommonJS).

## Architecture (reference for editing the kit)

* `web/index.html` loads `main.js` (ES module) over a local HTTP server.
* `main.js`: renderer, toon materials, world, cards (1200x2000 CanvasTexture planes, origin at soles), passes colour (RT samples 4) → normal/depth → ink+grade (saturation, gain/lift, vignette, flash) → DOF to screen. It exposes `window.renderFrame(f)` and `window.ready`.
* `story.js` exports SHOTS, `shotAt`, `camAt`, `dofAt`, LIGHTS/`lightFor`, `worldAt`, `actorsAt(f, T)`, `fxAt(f, T)`. All are pure functions of the frame (required: Modal renders chunks in separate browsers).
* `render.mjs`: Playwright → `renderFrame(f)` → JPEG → `<outdir>/f0000.jpg`.
* `timing.py`: word timings. `mix.py`: gated-RMS level lock. `modal_render.py`: cloud render + encode + QA + download. `finish.sh`: local encode fallback.
* Characters: the Pen API from mromidev/claude-animated-shorts `templates/bridge/toon.ts`, extended in the same module scope by the cast file.
